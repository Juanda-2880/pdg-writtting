# Defining Research Objectives (General and Specific)

## Overview

Research objectives represent the operational commitments of a study. While research questions define _what_ the inquiry seeks to understand, objectives define _what direct actions and targets_ the researcher will execute to answer those questions.

A thesis or research project stands or falls on the structural coherence between:
$$\text{Problem / Gap} \longrightarrow \text{Research Questions} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Objectives} \longrightarrow \text{Methodology} \longrightarrow \text{Results \& Conclusions}$$

This skill guides students and researchers through formulating, scoping, refining, and auditing both **General Objectives** (_Objetivo General_) and **Specific Objectives** (_Objetivos Específicos_).

---

## Core Principles & Criteria

### 1. General Objective vs. Specific Objectives

- **General Objective (1 single goal)**:
  - Answers the overarching research question.
  - Summarizes the core purpose and final deliverable/contribution of the research.
  - Sets the global scope and boundary of the study.
- **Specific Objectives (typically 3 to 5 sequentially ordered goals)**:
  - Serve as the tactical, intermediate milestones necessary and sufficient to achieve the general objective.
  - Follow an operational, chronological, or diagnostic-to-evaluative progression.
  - Each specific objective directly maps to a distinct methodological phase or chapter in the thesis.

### 2. SMART Objective Standards

Every objective must fulfill:

- **Specific (S)**: Narrow, unambiguous statement of action and object of study.
- **Measurable / Verifiable (M)**: Clear criteria or empirical output to determine whether it has been achieved.
- **Achievable / Attainable (A)**: Realistic within the student's resources, access to sample/data, timeframe, and institutional requirements.
- **Relevant (R)**: Directly targets the identified research problem and gap; does not drift into unrelated side inquiries.
- **Time-bound (T)**: Executable within the project or academic term constraints.

### 3. Syntactic Formula for Writing Objectives

Every objective must strictly follow this grammatical formulation:

$$\mathbf{Infinitive\ Action\ Verb} + \mathbf{Object\ of\ Study\ /\ Variable(s)} + \mathbf{Target\ Subject\ /\ Context} + \mathbf{Purpose\ /\ Method\ (How\ \&\ Why)}$$

_Example:_

> _"**Analizar** [Verb] el impacto de la arquitectura de microservicios en la latencia de respuesta [Object] en aplicaciones de banca móvil de alta concurrencia [Context] **a través de** pruebas de carga sintéticas y telemetría distribuida para optimizar la asignación de recursos [Purpose/How]."_

## Cognitive Depth & Bloom's Taxonomy Alignment

Objectives should employ actionable verbs suited for empirical research. Avoid vague, non-verifiable verbs like _"comprender"_, _"saber"_, _"estudiar"_, _"conocer"_, _"reflexionar"_, or _"profundizar"_.

### Recommended Action Verbs by Level:

1. **Diagnostic / Exploratory (Phase 1)**:
   - _Verbs_: Identificar, caracterizar, clasificar, diagnosticar, describir, documentar, registrar.
   - _Focus_: Baseline assessment, mapping current conditions, or determining starting parameters.
2. **Analytical / Relational / Comparative (Phase 2)**:
   - _Verbs_: Analizar, comparar, examinar, correlacionar, contrastar, descomponer, verificar.
   - _Focus_: Exploring relationships between variables, empirical testing, and differential analysis.
3. **Design / Development / Synthesis (Phase 3)**:
   - _Verbs_: Diseñar, formular, modelar, estructurar, desarrollar, implementar, construir.
   - _Focus_: Proposing architectures, intervention models, frameworks, or algorithms.
4. **Evaluation / Validation (Phase 4)**:
   - _Verbs_: Evaluar, validar, contrastar, estimar, medir, cuantificar, comprobar.
   - _Focus_: Measuring impact, validating hypotheses, running benchmark comparisons, or verifying performance against baselines.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The General Objective verb must represent a cognitive level equal to or higher than any of the Specific Objectives. A specific objective should not require a higher level of synthesis or evaluation than the general objective itself.

## Operational Modes

### Mode 0: Diagnostics & Alignment Check

Start here when the topic or research question is fuzzy or newly formed:

1. Extract or elicit the central problem statement, research questions, and proposed scope.
2. Verify alignment: Does each research question have a corresponding objective?
3. Check feasibility: Are the required variables measurable? Can data be gathered within the timeframe?

### Mode 1: Formulating the General Objective

1. Identify the core independent and dependent variables, unit of analysis, and target context.
2. Select an overarching terminal verb that reflects the final contribution (e.g., _Evaluar_, _Desarrollar_, _Determinar_, _Analizar_).
3. Draft a singular, powerful statement encapsulating the entire project's aim.

### Mode 2: Deriving Specific Objectives

Break the general objective down into 3 to 5 stepwise, chronological milestones:

1. **Objective 1 (Diagnostic/Foundational)**: Characterize the baseline, current state, or theoretical foundations.
2. **Objective 2 (Analytical/Experimental)**: Analyze, measure, or experiment on the key variables or relations.
3. **Objective 3 (Design/Implementation)**: Design, propose, or construct the solution, model, or protocol (if engineering/applied).
4. **Objective 4 (Evaluation/Validation)**: Evaluate, validate, or benchmark the results against the problem baseline.

### Mode 3: Audit & Checklist Review

Evaluate existing drafts against the following criteria:

- [ ] **Single action verb**: Does each objective start with exactly one infinitive verb?
- [ ] **No methodological task confusions**: Are literature searches, interviews, or survey administrations framed as tools/tasks rather than stand-alone research objectives? (e.g., Avoid _"Revisar la literatura..."_ or _"Aplicar 20 encuestas..."_ as objectives).
- [ ] **Exclusivity & Sufficiency**: If all specific objectives are accomplished, is the general objective fully satisfied? Does any specific objective exceed the scope of the general objective?
- [ ] **Verifiable outcome**: Can an external reviewer unequivocally point to concrete evidence/data showing the objective was achieved?
- [ ] **Direct mapping**: Does each specific objective correspond to a dedicated chapter/section in the Methodology and Results?

## Common Antipatterns to Avoid

| Anti-pattern                                                                           | Why it fails                                                                                                       | Corrective Action                                                                                     |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Methodological task as objective** (_"Hacer encuestas a 50 usuarios"_)               | Administering an instrument is an operational activity, not a scientific research aim.                             | Transform into the analytical aim: _"Determinar el nivel de satisfacción de los usuarios..."_         |
| **Vague / Unmeasurable verbs** (_"Aprender sobre...", "Comprender la importancia..."_) | Internal cognitive states cannot be empirically evaluated by a thesis committee.                                   | Replace with verifiable verbs: _"Analizar los factores determinantes...", "Evaluar la incidencia..."_ |
| **Compound objectives** (_"Analizar y diseñar y evaluar..."_)                          | Bundles multiple goals into one, muddying criteria for success and evaluation.                                     | Split into discrete, sequentially staged specific objectives.                                         |
| **Scope leap** (Specific objective demands more than the General)                      | Incoherent hierarchy (e.g., General is _"Describir..."_ while Specific is _"Validar el impacto econométrico..."_). | Elevate the General Objective verb or scope down the specific objective.                              |
| **Redundant specific objectives**                                                      | Objectives that repeat the same measurement under slight phrasing variations.                                      | Consolidate or re-align along sequential research phases.                                             |
