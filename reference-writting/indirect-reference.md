# Indirect Citations & Paraphrasing in Academic Writing (Citas Indirectas o Paráfrasis)

A comprehensive reference guide for synthesizing, paraphrasing, and integrating ideas, findings, and theories from external literature in theses, dissertations, and research papers according to APA 7th edition standards.

---

## 1. Overview & Methodological Superiority

An **indirect citation** (*cita indirecta o paráfrasis*) expresses an author's idea, theory, empirical finding, or conclusion in the researcher's own words while crediting the intellectual source.

### Why Paraphrasing is Superior in Empirical Research
- **Demonstrates Mastery:** Proves that the researcher understands the concept rather than mechanically copying strings of text.
- **Stylistic Uniformity:** Maintains a consistent academic voice, tone, and technical vocabulary across the thesis.
- **Analytical Synthesis:** Allows contrasting or consolidating findings from multiple studies into a single cohesive argument.
- **Conciseness:** Strips away non-essential narrative filler from original studies, distilling only the empirical outcome relevant to the research question.

---

## 2. Core Formatting Rules (APA 7th)

| Dimension | Rule & Specification |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Quotation Marks** | **NEVER use quotation marks** (`"..."`) for an indirect citation or paraphrase. |
| **Required Metadata** | Author surname(s) and publication year: `(Author, Year)` or `Author (Year)`. |
| **Page Numbers** | **Optional, but highly recommended** when referring to a specific hypothesis, table, theorem, or bounded section in a lengthy book or monograph (e.g., `Smith, 2020, p. 115`). Not required when referring to the general thesis or overarching study. |
| **Punctuation** | When parenthetical, place the period **outside** the closing parenthesis `(García, 2022).` |

## 3. Structural Variations of Indirect Citations

### A. Narrative Citation (Cita Narrativa)
The author's name is incorporated directly into the grammatical subject or narrative of the sentence, followed immediately by the year in parentheses.
- **Formula:** $\mathbf{Author} + \mathbf{(Year)} + \mathbf{Reporting\ Verb} + \mathbf{Paraphrased\ Synthesis}$
- **Best Used For:** Highlighting the specific researcher's contribution, establishing theoretical lineage, or discussing debates between authors.
- **Examples:**
  > Vaswani et al. (2017) propusieron la arquitectura Transformer, demostrando que los mecanismos de atención eliminan la necesidad de recurrencia en el procesamiento del lenguaje natural.
  >
  > Según argumentan Goodfellow y Bengio (2016), las redes generativas adversarias logran estabilizar su entrenamiento cuando se optimiza la divergencia de Jensen-Shannon.

### B. Parenthetical Citation (Cita Parentética)
Both the author's surname and the year appear together in parentheses at the end of the paraphrased statement or clause.
- **Formula:** $\mathbf{Paraphrased\ Proposition} + \mathbf{(Author,\ Year).}$
- **Best Used For:** Emphasizing the empirical finding, fact, or methodology itself without drawing primary attention to the identity of the author.
- **Examples:**
  > El despliegue de microservicios en clústeres de Kubernetes incrementa la observabilidad operativa a costa de introducir una mayor sobrecarga de serialización en la red (Hernández & Pérez, 2021).
  >
  > La latencia de respuesta en arquitecturas sin servidor depende críticamente del fenómeno de arranque en frío (cold start) (Amazon Web Services, 2023).

## 4. Multi-Source Synthesis (Sintetizar Múltiples Fuentes)

One of the greatest strengths of indirect citations is consolidating findings from several independent studies in a single parenthetical citation.

### Rules for Multi-Source Parenthetical Citations:
1. Place authors in **alphabetical order** by first author's surname (matching the Reference List order).
2. Separate distinct works with a **semicolon (`;`)**.
3. Group multiple works by the exact same author chronologically, separated by commas.

### Example:
> Diversas investigaciones han corroborado que la modularización arquitectónica disminuye la propensión a errores en etapas avanzadas del ciclo de software (Davis & Taylor, 2019; Gómez et al., 2022; Smith & Johnson, 2018).

## 5. Effective Academic Reporting Verbs for Paraphrasing

Vary your verbs to reflect the nuance and degree of commitment of the original author:

| Intent / Tone | Recommended Verbs (Spanish) | Recommended Verbs (English) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neutral Reporting** | Afirma, señala, indica, menciona, expone, describe | States, reports, describes, indicates, notes |
| **Empirical Finding** | Demuestra, evidencia, comprueba, constata, corrobora | Demonstrates, shows, confirms, establishes |
| **Theoretical Stance / Argument** | Postula, sostiene, plantea, argumenta, propone | Argues, posits, asserts, contends, proposes |
| **Critical / Evaluative** | Cuestiona, rebate, objeta, matiza, advierte | Critiques, disputes, cautions, qualifies |

## 6. Anti-patterns in Paraphrasing (What to Avoid)

| Anti-pattern | Description | Corrective Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The "Thesaurus Patchwrite"** | Swapping every third word with a synonym while keeping original sentence structure intact. | Read the source, close it, write the concept from memory, then verify technical accuracy. |
| **Missing the Boundary** | It is unclear where the cited author's idea ends and your own analysis begins. | Frame the paraphrase with a clear opening marker (*"De acuerdo con X..."*) and close with your own analytical commentary. |
| **Over-attribution in Every Sentence** | Repeating `(Gómez, 2020)` at the end of every sentence in a single unified paragraph. | Introduce the author once clearly at the beginning of the paragraph and maintain lexical continuity. |
| **Attribution Distortion** | Stating that the author proved something when they merely hypothesized or suggested it. | Match the epistemic hedging: use *sugiere*, *plantea*, or *indica* rather than *demuestra*. |

---

## 7. Audit Checklist for Indirect Citations

- [ ] Are quotation marks completely absent?
- [ ] Is the publication year present and accurately paired with the author?
- [ ] Does the citation format adhere to narrative `Author (Year)` or parenthetical `(Author, Year)` conventions?
- [ ] Are multiple citations inside parentheses alphabetized by surname and separated by semicolons?
- [ ] Does the paraphrase reflect the true spirit and nuance of the original source without patchwriting?
